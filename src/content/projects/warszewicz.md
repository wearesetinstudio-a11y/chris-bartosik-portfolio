overviewText: |-
  Józef Warszewicz was a 19th-century Polish botanist and explorer who braved extreme conditions across Central America. Despite the technological limits of his era, he documented thousands of plant species unknown to Europe, becoming a central figure in global botany. His research introduced the world to unique orchid species that remain a point of national pride for Panama today. I created a short historical video to honor his legacy and raise awareness of this unique connection between Poland and Panama. Instead of relying on dry textbook formats, I translated this history into the language of modern digital media. By combining generative AI tools, cinematic tropical imagery, and archival notes, I built a visual piece that immediately captures attention and bridges two distant worlds.

    title: Low attention spans and missing archival imagery
    text: |-
      The project came with distinct constraints. Due to short attention spans on social feeds, historical content often struggles to gain traction, meaning the first second had to hook the viewer instantly. This was made harder by a complete lack of color archival imagery—most records of Warszewicz's work consist only of black-and-white sketches and old notes. Lastly, the piece required cultural nuance. The narrative had to resonate authentically with audiences in both Central Europe and Latin America without sounding overly dramatic or biased.

    title: Immersive openings and generative world-building
    text: |-

To solve the asset shortage, I leveraged AI workflows to generate photorealistic sequences of 19th-century field research and wild landscapes, maintaining a consistent cinematic tone. I anchored the story around universal themes of passion, resilience, and respect for nature, making the botanist’s journey accessible to a global audience.
