overviewText: >-
  I have worked at PhotoAiD since the early steps of my career. I knew and
  understood the business from their startup stage until the semi-corporate,
  grown company. That’s when the kiosk project came out. PhotoAiD itself was
  focused on biometric photo businesses, but as we grew, we needed to extend
  into other zones of the photographic industry. Our eye went into printing
  kiosks. We noticed our competitors were stuck in technological, design, and
  usability agony. That is where we saw our opening, and we took it.
sections:
  - label: challange
    title: “All in one and better”
    text: |
      Uniqueness we were offering since the beginning from the competitors in the business proposal was the product itself. Our competitors had kiosks that offered just printing of images, documents, or fun photos. Different competitor, different product. We were offering everything in one place, and with functionalities nobody else had.

      The kiosk had to provide these products:

      - photocards of client moments, captured in a creative, memorial way with special designs dedicated to themes and topics
      - biometric photo printing from our other products like the PhotoAiD app by using a code or device connection
      - ai photocards that turn real moments of users into unique memories crafted and generated just for them based on the metadata of the photos provided
      - a4 paper printing for daily documents, dedicated to students running late to school printing homework, cvs, standard paperwork, and classic documents
      - classic photo printing of various sizes with a full editing experience

      How to deliver a better version of the market with a unique offer of products, services, and experience?

      The product had no base, no data. At the start, we had to work blind, creating based on external investigation and solid practices to provide the highest quality and experience. This would give users spectacular control and offer something new. We were entering a market full of big players with data and stable user groups. We had to match their reliability while offering a better experience so users would actively choose PhotoAiD kiosks.
    afterGroup: 1
  - label: research
    title: The market and competitive investigation
    text: >-
      Our research was based on analyzing the market and our main competitors.
      We searched through multiple flows of competitor kiosks.


      Their main problems were that machine flows, interfaces, and interactions
      with the user were technical, industrial, and boxy. They did not provide
      any emotional solutions to integrate the user with the interface or engage
      them with it.


      However, there was a great takeaway that we also highlighted: the
      simplicity of the single-task flows and the clarity of the interface.
    afterGroup: 2
  - label: strategy
    title: Moving the UX and UI beyond the standards
    text: >-
      As soon as we noticed the weaknesses and strengths of our competitors, we
      conducted a strategy that combined them. We had to provide extremely
      simple and seamless flows that gave maximal functionality and control over
      the process.


      Except for that, we needed to commercialize it and make it functional. The
      interface had to reach beyond the current standard, engaging users and
      providing an exceptional endorphin boost.
    afterGroup: 3
  - label: flow
    title: Dividing complex processes into small seamless tasks
    text: >-
      We had to break down complex procedures into short, manageable steps.
      Users standing at a public kiosk do not want to read long instructions or
      deal with cluttered screens.


      By simplifying each phase from file transfer to final payment, we kept
      users moving forward without friction. Even during longer background
      processes like AI image generation, dividing the flow into clear
      micro-tasks kept the experience predictable and kept users engaged until
      the print was ready.
    afterGroup: 4
  - label: interface
    title: Manageable, flexible, and user experience centered solutions
    text: >-
      We needed an interface that would be scalable by the business, not by the
      hardware device. Compared to our competitors, we made our interface
      flexible for business needs. Because we focused on a single device format,
      we could easily adapt design variations to match changing business
      requirements and seasonal updates.


      To achieve this, we chose React Native. This gave both the business and
      the design side much greater capabilities to build an interface that was
      fluid, testable, and quick to edit without limits.


      Thanks to this foundation, we designed a dedicated design system built for
      IoT products. It originated directly from the design systems I had
      previously created for other PhotoAiD products, keeping everything
      consistent across the entire ecosystem.
    afterGroup: 5
  - label: interaction
    title: The endorphin boost on every level
    text: >-
      As we always provided a seamless, spectacular experience in our previous
      PhotoAiD products, we saw a massive change in engagement and conversion.
      Since the kiosks became not just a biometric tool, but an occasional and
      emotional product, we designed plenty of interactions and motions. This
      provided an experience that boosted endorphins with every small task
      completed.
    afterGroup: 6
  - label: result
    title: The business gamechanger
    text: >-
      Expanding into this zone gave PhotoAiD a massive boost in technological,
      design, and business directions. A strong interface and user experience
      drove new partnerships and engaged new users who eventually converted into
      users of our other products as well.
